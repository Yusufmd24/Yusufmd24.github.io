Business Problem
In digital payment systems, fraud rarely announces itself through volume—it hides in patterns. With a fraud rate of just 0.17%, blanket detection rules fail: they either miss fraud entirely or generate so many false positives that risk teams are overwhelmed.

This project builds a pattern-based fraud intelligence system that identifies when, where, and how fraud concentrates—enabling risk teams to allocate monitoring resources with surgical precision rather than broad filtering.

Key Findings
Time-Based Concentration: 12AM–4AM
Fraud is heavily concentrated in the midnight-to-4AM window—deliberate exploitation of low-monitoring periods. This is a behaviorally timed pattern, not statistical noise. Detection models must weight time-of-day as a primary risk signal.
Category Skew: Grocery (~55% of Cases)
The Grocery category accounts for ~55% of all fraud—far exceeding its transaction share. This indicates either weaker card-present validation or higher exposure to stolen credential use in this category specifically.
Merchant Clustering: Swiggy & Zomato
Swiggy and Zomato emerge as leading merchant contributors to fraud transactions—consistent with high-frequency, low-friction environments where velocity is high and review windows are short. Merchant-specific risk scoring is warranted.
Priority-Ordered Recommendations
HIGH
Deploy real-time anomaly alerts for 12AM–4AM window—highest fraud ROI per monitoring hour
HIGH
Implement adaptive authentication for Grocery category—step-up verification above a transaction threshold
MED
Apply dynamic risk scoring to Swiggy & Zomato—merchant-specific flags feeding real-time decisioning
MED
Shift to combined signal detection—time + category + merchant triple-signal combination for precision targeting
LOW
Build investigator drillthrough workflows for fraud ops teams to query transaction-level detail without SQL access

Business Problem
Businesses with thousands of customers often treat them uniformly—applying the same marketing budget and retention effort regardless of actual value delivered.

This project proves that approach is wasteful. Using 150,000 transactions across 5,000 customers over 2 years, the analysis identifies who generates the most revenue, who is at churn risk, and how marketing budgets should be reallocated to maximise ROI across differentiated segments.
RFM Segmentation Query
T-SQLCustomer_segmentation.sql
-- RFM Segmentation with CTEs
WITH customer_metrics AS (
  SELECT
    customer_id,
    ROUND(SUM(total_amount), 2)  AS Monetary,
    COUNT(order_id)               AS Frequency,
    DATEDIFF(day, MAX(order_date),
             GETDATE())            AS Recency
  FROM orders
  GROUP BY customer_id
)
SELECT *,
  CASE
    WHEN Monetary  > 5000 AND Frequency > 10
         THEN 'High Value'
    WHEN Frequency > 8
         THEN 'Loyal'
    WHEN Recency   > 180
         THEN 'Churn Risk'
    ELSE 'Regular'
  END AS customer_segment
FROM customer_metrics;
Pareto Analysis (80/20 Rule)
T-SQLPareto_analysis.sql
WITH revenue_table AS (
  SELECT customer_id,
         SUM(total_amount) AS revenue
  FROM  orders
  GROUP BY customer_id
),
revenue_calc AS (
  SELECT customer_id, revenue,
         SUM(revenue) OVER()          AS total_revenue,
         revenue * 100.0
           / SUM(revenue) OVER()    AS revenue_pct
  FROM  revenue_table
)
SELECT * FROM revenue_calc
ORDER BY revenue DESC;
Key Findings
Pareto Law Confirmed
The top 20% of customers generate ~80% of total revenue. High-value customers (Monetary > 5,000 & Frequency > 10) form a small but critical retention target with outsized business impact.
Churn Risk Cohort Identified
A meaningful cohort has not purchased in over 180 days—at active churn risk but recoverable with the right re-engagement trigger within a 30-day window.
Frequency and Spend Are Decoupled
Loyal customers (Frequency > 8) are not always high spenders—frequency and monetary value are partially independent, requiring separate retention and upsell strategies per segment.
Business Recommendations
HIGH
Loyalty rewards programme for High Value segment—cost of retention is far lower than reacquisition
HIGH
Re-engagement campaign for Churn Risk cohort—targeted discount or personalised outreach within 30-day window
MED
Upsell to Loyal Customers—frequency is established; focus on increasing average order value
LOW
Reallocate marketing budget away from Regular segment toward High Value and Churn Risk tiers

Business Problem
The Indian hotel industry is highly fragmented. Pricing strategies, occupancy rates, and guest behaviour vary dramatically by region, category, and season—yet most operators make pricing decisions without structured data analysis.

This project applies exploratory data analysis to surface the patterns operators and investors need: which hotel categories maximise RevPAR, when demand peaks and troughs, and which regions are structurally under- or over-priced relative to occupancy signals.

Key Findings
Seasonal Demand Concentration
Occupancy shows strong seasonal concentration. Peak demand windows create pricing leverage that dynamic-pricing operators capture—while flat-rate hotels leave significant RevPAR on the table through the same periods.
Regional Price-Occupancy Mismatch
Several regions show high occupancy at low ADR—under-pricing relative to actual demand. Other regions show the inverse: premium pricing with below-average occupancy, suggesting brand overreach beyond addressable market.
Category Performance Divergence
Business hotel categories outperform on weekday occupancy, but leisure properties generate higher weekend RevPAR. Optimal portfolio strategy is location-type dependent, not universal.
Business Recommendations
HIGH
Implement dynamic pricing models for peak seasonal windows—static pricing leaves 15–25% RevPAR uncaptured
MED
Reprice under-priced high-occupancy regions—demand signal exists; pricing strategy is not capitalising on it
LOW
Differentiate weekday vs. weekend strategy for mixed business/leisure markets to maximise RevPAR across both segments
